<Callout kind="definition" title="In one line">
No wireless technology is best at everything: you choose by asking how far, how fast, how long on a battery, how cheap and how many devices, then pick the technology whose trade-offs fit.
</Callout>

## Why the choice matters

A soil sensor on a farm, a fitness band and a CCTV camera all need wireless links, but they need very different ones. The wrong choice means a battery that dies in a week, a signal that cannot reach, or a bill that is too high.

Every radio trades **range**, **data rate** and **power** against each other. Sending far needs more power or a slower signal. Sending fast needs more power or a shorter distance. That is why there are so many technologies.

<Callout kind="remember" title="Rules of thumb">
Longer range usually means a lower data rate. A higher data rate usually means more power. Mesh networks extend range hop by hop, but routers must stay powered.
</Callout>

## Choosing, step by step

1. **Rule out** anything that cannot reach far enough or carry the data. Live video rules out LoRaWAN and Zigbee; 5 km rules out everything except LPWAN or cellular.
2. **Check power.** For a battery that must last years, prefer BLE, Zigbee end devices or LoRaWAN, and avoid always-on Wi-Fi.
3. **Check topology.** Many devices spread through a building suit a mesh such as Zigbee or Thread.
4. **Check who must connect.** If a phone talks to the device directly, use BLE, NFC or Wi-Fi.
5. **Check IP.** If the device must speak IP without a gateway, use Wi-Fi, Thread (6LoWPAN) or cellular.

## Advantages and limitations of comparing by criteria

**Advantages**

- Turns a confusing list of standards into a few clear questions.
- Avoids over-paying for speed or range a project does not need.
- Makes trade-offs visible, so the choice can be explained to others.

**Limitations**

- Real range depends on walls, terrain and antennas, so typical values are only a guide.
- Some needs pull in different directions; there is rarely a perfect answer.
- Running costs, such as network fees, can matter as much as the radio.
