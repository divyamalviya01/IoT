<Callout kind="definition" title="In one line">
Bluetooth is a short-range 2.4 GHz radio in two forms: Bluetooth Classic streams data such as audio, while Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) sends small amounts of data at very low power, which suits most IoT devices.
</Callout>

## The Bluetooth SIG

The <Term id="bluetooth-sig">Bluetooth Special Interest Group (SIG)</Term> was founded in 1998 by Ericsson, IBM, Intel, Nokia and Toshiba. Today it has thousands of member companies. The SIG:

- writes and updates the **Bluetooth Core Specification**;
- defines **profiles** (for example, how headsets or heart-rate monitors behave);
- assigns **UUIDs**, such as 0x180D for the Heart Rate service;
- runs **qualification**, so only tested products may use the Bluetooth name and logo.

The IEEE once published early Bluetooth as IEEE 802.15.1, but that standard is no longer maintained; the SIG owns Bluetooth now. The name comes from Harald "Bluetooth", a Danish king who united tribes, just as Bluetooth unites devices.

## Bluetooth Classic

- Works at 2.4 GHz on **79 channels**, each 1 MHz wide.
- Uses <Term id="frequency-hopping">frequency hopping</Term>: it changes channel **1600 times a second**, so interference on one channel does little harm.
- Devices form a <Term id="piconet">piconet</Term>: one **central** device and up to **seven active peripherals**. Piconets that share a device form a **scatternet**.
- Data rate 1 Mbps (Basic Rate), up to 3 Mbps with Enhanced Data Rate.
- Typical uses: wireless earbuds and speakers, car hands-free kits, and older serial links.

## Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)

<Term id="ble">BLE</Term> is not just a lower-power Classic: it is a different radio protocol that shares the name and the band.

### Channels and advertising

BLE uses **40 channels**, each 2 MHz wide. Three of them, **37, 38 and 39**, are for <Term id="advertising">advertising</Term>. They sit at 2402, 2426 and 2480 MHz, in the gaps between Wi-Fi channels 1, 6 and 11, to avoid Wi-Fi. The other 37 carry data after a connection.

A BLE device that wants to be found sends a short advertisement on all three advertising channels, every few tens or hundreds of milliseconds. A phone scanning nearby hears it and can connect.

### GATT: services and characteristics

Once connected, a BLE device shows its data through <Term id="gatt">GATT</Term> (Generic Attribute Profile):

- A **service** groups related data, such as Heart Rate (UUID 0x180D) or Battery (0x180F).
- A <Term id="characteristic">characteristic</Term> is one value inside a service, such as Heart Rate Measurement (0x2A37) or Battery Level (0x2A19).
- Each characteristic has **properties**: read, write and notify.
- To receive updates automatically, the phone writes `01 00` to the characteristic's <Term id="cccd">CCCD</Term>; the device then **notifies** it whenever the value changes.

### Beacons

A <Term id="beacon">beacon</Term> is a BLE device that only advertises a short ID, such as Apple's iBeacon or Google's Eddystone. A phone that hears the ID knows it is near a certain shop shelf or museum exhibit. Phones estimate the distance from the signal strength (<Term id="rssi">RSSI</Term>), which is only rough: walls and bodies weaken the signal. Lab L17 shows how big the error can be.

## Bluetooth Mesh

Bluetooth Mesh (2017) connects hundreds of BLE devices, mostly for lighting. It uses **managed flooding**: a message is rebroadcast by relay nodes until its hop limit runs out, rather than following a fixed route as in Zigbee.

<Callout kind="example" title="A fitness band and earbuds">
A fitness band uses BLE: it advertises, the phone connects, and the band notifies heart-rate readings every second while lasting a week on a tiny battery. Wireless earbuds use Bluetooth Classic (or the newer LE Audio) because audio needs a continuous stream.
</Callout>

## Advantages and limitations

**Advantages**

- Built into every smartphone, so no extra gateway is needed for personal devices.
- BLE uses very little power and connects in milliseconds.
- Standard profiles and SIG qualification help devices work together.

**Limitations**

- Short range: usually 10–30 m indoors.
- Shares the crowded 2.4 GHz band.
- Distance estimates from RSSI are rough.
- Needs a phone or gateway to reach the internet.
