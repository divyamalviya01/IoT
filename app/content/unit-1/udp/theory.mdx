<Callout kind="definition" title="In one line">
UDP sends packets straight away with no connection and no delivery checks, which makes it fast and light; TCP sets up a connection and guarantees every byte arrives in order, which costs time and extra messages.
</Callout>

## Two ways to deliver data

The transport layer carries data between programs on two devices. It uses a <Term id="port">port</Term> number to find the right program, such as port 1883 for an MQTT broker.

The internet offers two transport protocols, and they make opposite choices:

- <Term id="udp">UDP</Term> (User Datagram Protocol) is like posting a postcard. You drop it in the box and hope it arrives.
- <Term id="tcp">TCP</Term> (Transmission Control Protocol) is like a registered parcel with tracking. Every item is confirmed, and anything lost is sent again.

## How UDP works

UDP sends each message as a separate packet called a datagram. There is no setup and no reply.

- **Connectionless:** the first datagram carries data immediately.
- **No delivery guarantee:** lost datagrams are not resent.
- **No ordering:** datagrams can arrive in a different order.
- **Tiny header:** only 8 bytes.

## How TCP works

TCP first opens a connection with a three-way <Term id="handshake">handshake</Term>. Then it numbers every byte, waits for an <Term id="acknowledgement">acknowledgement (ACK)</Term> and resends anything not acknowledged in time.

TCP also controls the flow of data so a fast sender does not swamp a slow receiver, and it slows down when the network is congested. Its header is at least 20 bytes.

## When does IoT use UDP?

UDP suits IoT when messages are small and frequent, and an occasional loss does not matter:

- A temperature sensor sends a reading every 10 seconds. If one is lost, the next one arrives soon.
- Battery devices save energy by skipping the handshake and ACKs.
- CoAP runs over UDP and adds its own light reliability only for messages that need it.

TCP suits IoT when every message matters, such as a payment, a firmware update or a command to unlock a door.

<Callout kind="example" title="Live cricket on your phone">
Video streaming and voice calls use UDP. A lost packet means a tiny glitch, which is better than the whole video pausing while TCP waits for a resend. IoT sensors that send frequent readings make the same trade-off.
</Callout>

## Advantages and limitations of UDP

**Advantages**

- Very small 8-byte header and no handshake, so less data and less energy.
- No waiting for acknowledgements, so lower delay.
- Supports broadcast and multicast: one packet can reach many devices.

**Limitations**

- Lost packets are not resent unless the application does it.
- No ordering, flow control or congestion control.
- Applications must handle any reliability they need themselves.
