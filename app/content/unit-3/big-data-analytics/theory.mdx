<Callout kind="definition" title="In one line">
Big data is data too large, too fast or too varied for ordinary databases and single computers; big data analytics finds useful patterns and decisions in it.
</Callout>

## Why IoT creates big data

One sensor sending one reading a minute is small. But IoT systems have very many devices, all sending all the time.

<Callout kind="example" title="Smart meters add up">
Suppose 10 lakh (1 million) smart meters each send a reading every 15 minutes: 96 readings a day. That is 9.6 crore (96 million) readings a day. At about 100 bytes each, it is roughly 9.6 GB a day, or about 3.5 TB a year, from meter readings alone. India is installing crores of smart meters, so the real numbers are far larger.
</Callout>

Data at this scale needs special tools to store and process it. That is <Term id="big-data">big data</Term>.

## The 5 Vs of big data

The idea began with three Vs (volume, velocity, variety), described by analyst Doug Laney in 2001. Veracity and value were added later. Together they are the <Term id="five-vs">5 Vs</Term>.

## Simple stream analytics

Two simple tools catch many problems in sensor streams. Lab L22 lets you try both.

- **<Term id="moving-average">Moving average</Term>:** the average of the last N readings. It smooths out noise, but trails real changes by about half the window.
- **<Term id="z-score">Z-score</Term>:** how unusual a reading is compared with recent ones:

  z = (reading − average of recent readings) ÷ standard deviation of recent readings

  If the last 20 readings average 28 °C with a standard deviation of 0.3 °C, a reading of 36 °C has z ≈ 27, far above the usual limit of 3. It is an <Term id="anomaly">anomaly</Term>.

A z-score catches sudden spikes, but misses a slow drift, because the average drifts too. A stuck sensor gives the same value again and again, so it needs its own rule.

## Challenges

- **Cost:** storing and processing terabytes costs money.
- **Quality (veracity):** faulty sensors and gaps give wrong answers.
- **Privacy:** meter data can reveal when a family is at home.
- **Speed:** some answers are only useful within seconds.
- **Skills:** analytics needs people who understand both data and the domain.
