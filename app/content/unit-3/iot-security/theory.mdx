<Callout kind="definition" title="In one line">
IoT security means protecting connected devices, their networks, data and cloud services so that only the right people and devices can use them, and nothing is read, changed or stopped without permission.
</Callout>

## Why IoT security matters

A hacked laptop leaks data. A hacked IoT device can also act in the real world: unlock a door, switch off a factory pump or flood a website with traffic. And there are billions of IoT devices, many of them cheap and rarely updated.

## The goals of security

The classic goals are the <Term id="cia-triad">CIA triad</Term>. IoT systems need three more.

<Term id="authentication">Authentication</Term> asks "who are you?"; <Term id="authorisation">authorisation</Term> asks "what may you do?"; <Term id="non-repudiation">non-repudiation</Term> keeps proof of what was done.

## Why IoT devices are easy targets

- **Weak defaults:** many devices ship with the same factory password, such as admin / admin.
- **Small and cheap:** little memory and CPU leave less room for strong security.
- **Rarely updated:** devices run for 10 years or more, often with no automatic updates.
- **Physically reachable:** a sensor on a pole or a lock on a door can be opened and tampered with.
- **Huge numbers of identical devices:** one flaw works on every copy at once.
- **Many makers and protocols:** devices, gateways, apps and clouds from different companies must all be secure.

Together these make a large <Term id="attack-surface">attack surface</Term>: every device, radio, port, app and account is a possible way in. The Visualize tab shows it on a 3D smart home, layer by layer.

## OWASP IoT Top 10 (2018)

The Open Worldwide Application Security Project (OWASP) lists the ten most common IoT weaknesses. The 2018 list is still the latest IoT edition.

## Laws and guidelines

- **India:** the Telecommunication Engineering Centre published a *Code of Practice for Securing Consumer IoT* (TEC 31318:2021) in August 2021, based on the European standard ETSI EN 303 645. It asks for unique passwords, a way to report flaws, and secure updates. CERT-In is India's agency for reporting cyber incidents.
- **United Kingdom:** since 29 April 2024 smart devices may not be sold with guessable default passwords (the PSTI Act).
- **European Union:** under the Cyber Resilience Act, makers must report actively exploited flaws from 11 September 2026, and meet all its security rules from 11 December 2027.
- **United States:** the Cyber Trust Mark is a voluntary label for smart products that meet security criteria.

<Callout kind="example" title="A housing society's cameras">
A housing society in Pune installs 32 CCTV cameras and a video recorder, and nobody changes the default password. The recorder is reachable from the internet through the router. Within weeks it is found by automated scanners and joins a botnet. Changing the password, turning off remote access through the router and updating the firmware would have stopped it.
</Callout>

## Building security in

- **Security by design:** plan security from the first day, not after a breach.
- **Defence in depth:** several layers, so one failure does not open everything.
- **Least privilege:** each user, device and app gets only the access it needs.
- **Secure by default:** safe settings out of the box.
- **Update for life:** signed updates for as long as the device is in use.
