## Messages travel through open air

A Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or LoRa message is a radio signal. Anyone within range with a cheap receiver can record it. On the internet, a message also passes through many routers owned by other people.

## How encryption helps

Encryption turns <Term id="plaintext">plaintext</Term> into ciphertext using a key. Without the key, the ciphertext is useless.

- **Confidentiality:** eavesdroppers see only random-looking bytes.
- **Integrity:** modern encryption adds an authentication tag (for example AES-GCM). If even one bit changes, the receiver rejects the message.
- **Authentication:** certificates, used in TLS, prove that the server, and sometimes the device, is genuine.
- **Freshness:** sequence numbers inside the protocol make replayed messages fail.

## What encryption does not hide

- **Metadata:** who talks to whom, when and how much. A burst of traffic at 7 a.m. can still show that someone woke up.
- **A hacked device:** if the attacker controls the device, they see the data before it is encrypted.
- **Weak passwords and keys:** encryption with a key everyone knows protects nothing.
- **Bad certificate checks:** if a device accepts any certificate, a man-in-the-middle can still read everything.

## Privacy laws

Much IoT data is personal: location, health readings, voices and daily routines. Laws require it to be protected.

- **India: Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.** Personal data needs consent for a clear purpose and "reasonable security safeguards". Breaches must be reported to the Data Protection Board and to the people affected. Failing to protect data can cost up to ₹250 crore. The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025 and apply in stages until May 2027.
- **European Union: GDPR (2018).** Similar duties, with fines up to €20 million or 4 % of worldwide turnover.

Encryption is one of the clearest "reasonable safeguards" a company can show.

<Callout kind="example" title="What a smart meter reveals">
A smart electricity meter reports every 15 minutes. Without encryption, anyone listening can see when the family wakes up, when the house is empty and even which appliances they use. Encrypting the readings keeps this private; collecting only what the bill needs reduces the risk further.
</Callout>

## Why IoT encryption is harder

- Small devices have little CPU, memory and battery (topic 3.11 covers lightweight ciphers).
- Every device needs its own keys, delivered and stored safely (topics 3.10 and 3.14).
- Devices last for years, so the encryption must stay strong and be updatable.
