<Callout kind="definition" title="In one line">
Cloud computing means renting computing power, storage and software over the internet, when you need it, and paying only for what you use.
</Callout>

## What is cloud computing?

Before the cloud, a company that wanted a website or a database bought its own servers. It needed a room, power, cooling and staff to look after them. If demand grew, it had to buy more servers and wait weeks.

With the <Term id="cloud">cloud</Term>, a provider such as Amazon, Microsoft or Google runs huge data centres. Customers rent a slice of them over the internet in minutes, and hand it back when they no longer need it.

The standard definition comes from the US National Institute of Standards and Technology (**NIST SP 800-145**, 2011). In simple words: cloud computing gives on-demand network access to a shared pool of computing resources that can be set up and released quickly, with very little effort from the provider.

## Service models: IaaS, PaaS and SaaS

The service models differ in **how much the provider manages for you**. The Visualize tab shows this as a stack of layers that changes owner as you switch models.

- <Term id="iaas">IaaS</Term> is like renting an empty flat: you bring the furniture.
- <Term id="paas">PaaS</Term> is like a furnished flat: you just move in with your things.
- <Term id="saas">SaaS</Term> is like a hotel room: everything is done for you.

- **Scale:** a city may have lakhs of smart meters. The cloud can accept all their messages without the city buying servers.
- **Storage:** years of readings are needed for bills, trends and audits.
- **Analytics:** finding patterns, predicting faults and training machine-learning models needs a lot of computing power.
- **Access from anywhere:** users, apps and other systems reach the data over the internet.
- **Pay as you go:** a project can start small and grow without a large up-front cost.

The cloud is far away, so a round trip takes tens or hundreds of milliseconds. Jobs that must react in a few milliseconds, such as stopping a machine, run at the edge instead (topic 1.4).

<Callout kind="example" title="Smart electricity meters">
An electricity distribution company in India installs smart meters that report every 15 minutes. The readings go through mobile data to a cloud platform. The cloud stores them, works out each bill, spots meters that may be tampered with, and lets each family see its use in an app. The company did not have to build a data centre for this.
</Callout>

## Advantages and limitations

**Advantages**

- No large up-front cost for servers; pay only for what you use.
- Scale up or down within minutes.
- The provider handles hardware, power, cooling and backups.
- Services are reachable from anywhere.

**Limitations**

- Needs a reliable internet connection.
- Latency is too high for very fast control loops.
- Running costs grow with use and can surprise you.
- Vendor lock-in: moving to another provider can be hard.
- Data privacy and location: personal data must follow laws such as India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
