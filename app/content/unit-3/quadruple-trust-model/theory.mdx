<Callout kind="definition" title="In one line">
The Quadruple Trust Model says that people can trust an IoT system only when four qualities hold together: protection, security, privacy and safety.
</Callout>

## Where the idea comes from

**IoT-A** (Internet of Things Architecture) was a European research project (2010–2013). It produced an *Architectural Reference Model*: a common way to describe any IoT system, with models of its devices, information, functions and communication. One of its functional groups is security: authentication, authorisation, identity management, key exchange, and trust and reputation. It also asks designers to look at every system from a "trust, security and privacy" point of view. The <Term id="iot-a">IoT-A</Term> model was introduced in topic 2.3.

The **IEEE 2413** standard (approved in 2019, published in 2020; developed as IEEE P2413) defines an architecture framework for IoT. Its working group named a quality goal it called **"quadruple" trust**: protection, security, privacy and safety. Textbooks teach this as the <Term id="quadruple-trust">Quadruple Trust Model</Term> for IoT-A style architectures.

## They overlap, and sometimes pull against each other

- **Safety against security:** a smart lock that opens when its battery dies keeps people safe in a fire, but is less secure.
- **Security against privacy:** keeping detailed logs helps investigate attacks, but logs about people are personal data.
- **Security against safety:** encryption adds delay; a safety stop on a machine must act instantly, so it may use a separate, simpler channel.
- **Protection and security overlap:** availability is part of security, but a power cut or flood is not an attack. Protection covers the accidents too.

A good design names these trade-offs and decides each one deliberately.

## Trust between people and between devices

- **People** trust a system when it keeps them safe, keeps their data private and keeps working.
- **Devices** must also trust each other: a gateway should accept data only from devices it has authenticated, and may track each device's reputation, ignoring one that keeps sending impossible readings.
